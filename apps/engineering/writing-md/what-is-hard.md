> What’s hard nowadays?

Someone asked me that a few days ago, while I was talking about how much the software engineering job has changed this last year. With coding agents, coding isn’t hard anymore: the technical problems you usually run into on a regular SaaS don’t feel complicated, the agents just figure them out. So I wasn't sure about the answer, I mean, there are whole other aspects of software development that haven't been solved, those surely remain hard, but a good bunch of the complexity that we as software developers were tackling is now handled by the agents, and they are arguably making better decisions than us in many cases.

So, what's hard? Well, many of us still try to understand what the agents are doing, have some mental model of how the system is modeled and keep an understanding of the architecture. We often influence it, but the truth is that what agents suggest is usually good, or at least that’s how it looks, judging by how fast we accept it.

So, what’s hard? Well, to stop, read, understand, ask, challenge and change direction when needed. Basically, to not mindlessly accept what the agent proposes and not get [choice-architected](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/psychology/choice-architecture) by it. That’s hard. Why? I think it is actually related to our attention span, we are presented with what looks like a wall of text and we unconsciously surrender. We skim, we don't read, we don't form a mental model of the system and how this change modifies it. We want to blast through it and get this thing built, we do this over and over.

When I was working on how to get engineers to effectively work with agents I wanted them to be accountable for the decisions the agents were making, and for that I needed them to be aware of them, so I spent hours thinking on how to reduce that wall of text to its minimal expression so they wouldn't be tempted to just skim and continue. While I was doing that, I realised I was contributing to making their attention span shorter. I was tiktoking them, if you know what I mean. So I was faced with a contradiction: maybe I’d succeed at getting people to understand the decisions, but I’d also be contributing to the erosion of their attention spans.

I think we humans have a tendency to try to make everything easy, smooth, comfortable, we get so obsessed that we lose perspective on why that is good for us, not only for the business, but for us. Maybe we will make everything so easy that we will end up going to gyms for the mind the same way we artificially engineer gyms and workouts to compensate for all the physical effort that we are meant to do and are not doing anymore, wouldn't that be ironic?
