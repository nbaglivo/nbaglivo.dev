---
title: "My AI workflow got obsolete again"
date: "Oct 21, 2026"
---

Damn, this keeps happening... Anthropic made my workflows obsolete, again. I was brainstorming something in Claude Code, started from a vague idea, got it to investigate things and I finally came up with something more solid, so I asked Claude Code to spec it, thinking it would give me something like a PRD, and from there I could run it through my usual workflow, which involves gathering more technical details, making decisions on the architectural level, and slicing the project into vertical milestones that I could build individually. Turns out Claude Code gave a spec I could annotate, plannotator style. This made it really easy to review decision by decision, inquire about some things, decide on others, send to research on specific points. It also gave me vertical milestones that I could run, I just needed to ask it for ways of verifying these milestones... I asked the agent to build a `slice-build-verify` skill so I could have a process where an adversarial agent would review the milestone. At the end I had a concrete and well scoped plan, all key decisions explicit, a clear milestone list and how to verify it. Much of my harness was not needed anymore, it was absorbed by the model and the tool.

One thing though, I wasn't satisfied with the architecture description level. I want to be able to understand the systems I build, so before sending something to build, I need to have an understanding of what to expect to come out on the other end, and I did not have it. So I asked the Agent to explain what was decided on the spec and what would need to be decided by the coding agent, this gave me architectural decisions to be aware of and challenge, so I challenged some until I was satisfied. During this process I decided to bring back the ADR concept, so I modified the `slice-build-verify` skill to instruct coding agents to document decisions they made and if those would contradict anything on the spec then request human authorization first.

Almost there, but I still could not figure out the shape of the code structure, I wanted to know what components this thing would be generating, what abstractions, so I asked for this to be included in the Spec, so I could annotate and challenge again... I'm starting to see a pattern: the mindset is "Inquire & Challenge", or more poetically "be curious, keep pulling from the thread". So after this new interaction, I asked the model “most things came from you, what did I actually add?” The answer was that the model supplied the content and I supplied the structure: decision gates, early checks, verification, the settled-vs-open split, components, workflows. I didn’t make the decisions; I decided which questions had to be answered.

So I ended up rebuilding the harness, a thinner one, just two skills: [slice-build-verify](https://github.com/nbaglivo/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/slice-build-verify/SKILL.md) and [build-spec](https://github.com/nbaglivo/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/build-spec/SKILL.md). 


During this session, all research and most decisions were made by the model, I was mostly making sure I understood the decisions and they were sound, this is a bit different from other workflows I had, where I decided myself on architecture and let the model fill the smaller gaps, the implementation. I'm quite fed up with the comparison "A coding agent is like your minion, your junior developer" but with the newer models I feel I can let go more on these decisions and be just pulling threads... like I would with a senior developer who is solving a problem and presenting their plan to me, I'd just add questions... this felt like that, asking questions on the spec resembled that process, there's a risk I did not ask about everything and some decisions were not properly reviewed, but that's fine, it does not need to be perfect and I was running out of energy, so I reviewed what felt more important, using my senior developer intuition. 

So, conclusions? I have a new harness to experiment with, and I’m more open to rethinking how I work and letting go of some things. 

One thing that felt different about this session, compared with other workflows, was the feeling of flow. I was engaged with the thinking: asking things, pulling on threads, moving through the spec, annotating, doing one pass, then a second, a third even. I didn’t find myself in time lagoons, waiting for something to happen, context-switching to unrelated things, or pulling out my phone (okay, maybe once or twice).

I’m also as tired as if I’d coded the whole system. Time to close the laptop, though I’ll leave an agent building from the spec while I’m gone.
